## How to preserve and frame a puzzle

I first wrote these notes in 1999 and updated them in 2019.

### Supplies

1. **Frame**
   - Puzzles tend to fit standard sizes: 16x20, 18x24, 22x28, 24x36.
   - Bring a tape measure to Goodwill, the Salvation Army, and similar shops.
   - Buy glass. Plastic is terrible.
   - If you want to spend more: buy metal frame kits from a craft store (wait for discounts), or visit a framing supply store for non-glare glass. You will need cardboard or foam core for backing.
2. **Glue**
   - Milton Bradley Puzzle Preserver (toy stores, Walmart, Target).
   - Elmer's Glue or rubber cement for the backing.
3. **Wax paper**
4. **Poster board**
   - It comes in 22x28, a perfect size.
   - Not all "blacks" are black, but black is the backing I recommend.
   - Go for broke and get colored backing.
   - Some places will heat-seal your artwork to foam board.
   - You can also get a custom frame or matting for a perfect fit.
5. **Framing equipment**
   - Light to medium picture wire.
   - Eyehooks or hangers ([D-ring "mirror hangers"](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B019QX7CY8)).
   - Wall hangers and nails.
6. **Paper towels**
7. **Tools**
   - Hammer, screwdrivers (Phillips and slotted), wire cutters, pliers.
   - Staple gun, or a [much fancier point driver](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074WDPWQD).
   - A large nail to start the eyehooks.
   - Utility knife and scissors.
   - A long metal straightedge for cutting poster board (worth the cost), plus a ruler.
   - Glass cleaner.
   - Paint scraper, in case of price tags on frames.
   - Pencils.
   - A [self-healing cutting mat](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01BTFEMAI).
   - Phone books, textbooks, or anything big, flat and heavy.

### Steps

1. **Do the puzzle.**
   - Build it on poster board so it's easy to move.
   - If you built it on a bare table, try to slide poster board underneath.
2. **Glue the puzzle.**
   - Clear a large area and lay down overlapping sheets of wax paper, larger than the puzzle.
   - Turn the puzzle upside down. You can glue the picture side, but then the corners and edges curl up, and after the glue dries the center pushes against the glass.
   - To flip it, press the puzzle between two sheets of poster board or cardboard, like a sandwich, and turn it over **quickly**. Otherwise the puzzle slides out and goes flying.
   - Slide it onto the wax paper and dust off puzzle dust and crumbs.
   - Spread glue evenly over the entire surface. Let it seep into the cracks, but don't apply it too thickly.
   - Let it dry for a few hours. Optionally, flatten the puzzle with weights.
3. **Prepare the puzzle.**
   - Peel off the wax paper and trim the edges if glue seeped out.
   - Wipe the picture side with dry paper towels.
   - Check for globs of dried glue on the surface and peel them off.
4. **Prepare the frame.**
   - Take the backing out of the frame, prying up staples or points with a screwdriver or pliers.
   - Clean the inside of the glass. Beware of specks and dust: they will bug you forever.
   - Cut the poster board to the *interior* frame size on the cutting mat. I usually measure with the backing board, but that's not always a great fit.
   - Test the poster board in the frame, then test the puzzle in the frame. Puzzles are often larger than the size on the box, so you may need to trim an edge or choose a larger frame. Trimming a puzzle edge takes a very sharp utility knife.
5. **Glue the puzzle onto the board.**
   - Center the puzzle on the poster board. I usually eyeball it and adjust after gluing, but it's better to pencil light marks just under the puzzle's edges.
   - If you marked the board, apply a thin coat of glue everywhere inside the marks, staying away from the edges to avoid oozing. If you didn't, apply the glue to the puzzle.
   - Place the puzzle lightly, then quickly check all edges and corners and adjust by sliding gently. Slide too far and the glue will ooze and streak.
   - When you're satisfied, push down from the center and smooth outwards. You may want to lay heavy objects all over the surface.
   - Let it dry for a while (overnight if you're patient).
6. **Frame it.**
   - Carefully blow any specks off the glass, and clean it again if needed.
   - Place the puzzle in the frame, minding "up" and "down" if the frame isn't symmetric. Take care not to pull up the edges.
   - Put the frame backing on top, then look carefully at the picture side. Does everything look OK? Any specks of dust?
   - Fasten the backing with the staple gun or point driver, or push the original staples or points back into place.
7. **Add the hanging wire.**
   - Decide which side is up and turn the frame over.
   - On the back, measure a quarter to a third of the way down each side and mark a spot for an eyehook.
   - Tap a starter hole with a large nail and screw in the eyehook. Repeat at the same height on the other side.
   - Twist the wire through one eyehook, leaving 1–2 inches extra, and twirl the extra around the main wire.
   - Leave enough wire between the eyehooks to give 1–2 inches of slack, but not so much that the wire rises above the top of the frame.
   - Twirl the wire tight on the other eyehook, again with 1–2 inches extra.
8. **Pick a cool spot on the wall** and tap in the wall hanger.
9. **Hang and level the frame.** Stand back and enjoy. Congratulations!
