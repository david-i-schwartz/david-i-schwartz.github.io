Course development

Rochester Institute of Technology

IGME 689 (arcades) 2026–present
Arcade Game design and development: many “game students” just don’t seem to choose game-adjacent course offerings. So, I’m leveraging my professional experience to run something that will help students transition away from their notions of games with something immediately adjacent.
IGME 309 (revisited) 2024–present
In Fall 2024, I rewrote the course to remove OpenGL, which had been outdated for a long time, and replace it with OpenFrameworks. I submitted a new course proposal, which RIT approved in Spring 2025.
IGME 599/799 (3D real-time workflow) 2024
In 2024, I plan to expand my game physics course and IGME 309 development into a 3D workflow via NVIDIA Omniverse. This work will extend ATLAS (bit.ly/programgames) and the 2023 project with MITRE and RIT Innovation Fellows.
IGME 750 2024
Took over Graduate Game Engines when a faculty member was on medical leave. Working with two industry experts, we restructured the course to include more examples, new assignments, and weekly lecture topics.
IGME 206 2022–2023
Defined a relatively new bridge course for graduate and transfer students. The course material combines classic CS1 and CS2 material in the context of game design and development.
IGME 099 IGME 299 2014–2020
Developed and refined a co-op/career skills course for sophomores. The course involved collaborating with Career Services to explain how to create job-search materials. IGME-299 merged into IGME-099, which became the template for all GCCIS co-op prep classes and has helped thousands of students.
IGME 590 IGME 790 2014–2019
“Game Physics/Physically based Animation” and related game development examples. See also bit.ly/programgames.
IGME 695 2025
Rebuilt the course to include student engagement with guest speakers. IGM had not reviewed 695 since 2013.
IGME 797 (graduate bridge) 2014–2018
Developed a new course to help incoming MS-GDD students develop C, C++, graphics, architecture, and game physics.
Various courses 2010–2016
Semester conversion for undergraduate and graduate IGM courses: I played a crucial role in conversion planning (and eventually “fixing” as undergraduate program coordinator) for several IGM courses.
IGME 209 IGME 309 2011–2013 2024
Rewrote the entire “DSA” sequence: updated fixed-function material to the programmable pipeline via shaders, modern OpenGL, and C++. In 2024, I plan to redevelop 309 with OpenFrameworks to provide an “on ramp” to facilitate student success.
4080-221 4080-222 4080-223 2011–2012
Updated the entire introductory programming sequence (final year of the quarter system). Highlights include formalizing an “advanced” course for students with high CS-AP scores (which I handed off to other faculty after I joined administration) and rewriting all course projects, which created multiple portfolio items for my cohort of students.
4085-787 4085-788 2010–2012
Updated and formalized the MS-GDD capstone policies, documents, and procedures. See Section 11 for a comprehensive list of supported graduate projects/theses.
N/A 2009–2012
Developed courses on the ethics of sustainability using educational games based on economic game theory, funded by a Provost-awarded $22K grant. This work involved a multidisciplinary group of faculty and students from Sustainability, Philosophy, and IGM.
N/A 2009–2010
Collaborated with the faculty of IGM and RIT’s Electrical Engineering Technology department to write a CCLI proposal for Physical Computing 2, a follow-up to IGM’s Physical Computing course.
4080-221 4080-222 4080-223 2009–2010
Developed a compiler design project and introductory software engineering materials for this project-based development course. In Fall 2006, I also created a second game development project for the Game Boy Advance.
4002-590 4002-790 2008–2009
Developed a course on alternative game interfaces in which students make custom game controllers.
4080-330 2007–2008
Redesigned course; used by additional instructors.
4002-217 4002-218 2007–2008
Revamped the course material, leveraging my Cornell introductory programming experience.

Cornell University

CIS 300 CIS 400 2001–2007
My team and I developed two game design courses, CIS300 and CIS400, which comprise the core of Cornell’s Minor in Game Design. The minor is the first-ever undergraduate Ivy League program in game design. gdiac.cis.cornell.edu.
CS 212 2002–2007
Developed a compiler design project and introductory software engineering materials for this project development course. In the Fall of 2006, I created a second game development project for the Game Boy Advance.
CS 214 CS 215 2003–2007
Created two courses, Advanced UNIX (214) and Introduction to C# (215), and trained graduate students to administer them.
CS 100M 2000–2004
Developed the Academic Excellence Workshop program for CS100M in collaboration with Cornell’s College of Engineering.
CS 100M 2000
Developed course material for the first offering of CS100M, an introductory programming course that teaches programming with MATLAB and Java using scientific computing examples.

State University of New York at Buffalo

EAS 140 1997–1998
Developed Unix and Maple laboratory modules for EAS 140, an introductory engineering course. These modules formed the basis for the two textbooks I published during graduate school, ultimately helping me make my “break” into Cornell.
