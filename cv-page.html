3. Administrative accomplishments

As Director of the School of Interactive Games and Media (IGM) (2015–2026)

  • Leading a top-ranked game design and development program: US News & World Report (2026 Ranking: #3), Princeton Review: #4 undergraduate (2026), #4 graduate (2026), and Animation Career Review (#5 overall for 2025, 2026 rankings forthcoming). The rankings reflect my data collection, marketing, and program direction. This success stems from a combination of efforts and initiatives: successful staff and faculty hiring, “The Great Alumni Project” (we’ve tracked nearly 1,500 graduates), company outreach, my alumni network, faculty mentoring, increased scholarship and funding, students winning competitions, and overall job placement.
  • Founding of the university-wide initiative, RIT Games (games.rit.edu): Working with staff and faculty across RIT, I spearheaded the creation of a synchronized, growing portfolio of interlocked game and games-related academic programs. Our first parallel cohort of “Games Arts” students started in 2024, and IGM created two new minors (Game Design and Game Development) to add pathways and bridges for students across RIT.
  • Growing research funding and scholarly work: since I began in 2015, IGM faculty have raised approximately $8 million in funding, supporting hundreds of students and creating the Niantic GeoGames Lab (now renamed the Digital Twins Initiative Lab) and the MAGIC building.
  • Advancing all of RIT with new, multidisciplinary research areas: Growing the geogames efforts in IGM led to www.rit.edu/digitaltwins. I believe this area will further help connect engineering, science, business, art, and design, all infused with data science and AI.
  • Helped to found RIT esports, which is one of the leading collegiate esports programs.
  • Created and enhanced an administrative “backend”: chair/director guides, timelines, and “faculty-student-load” spreadsheets for load and enrollment projections for my college and RIT. RIT’s new chairs and directors use my guides, which means I’m helping all of RIT. Now that I have ingested my 55+ page document into NotebookLM and trained it, I have videos, slide decks, and an “RIT Employee Help Prompt,” which RIT and I are growing into a continuously updated help system.

As Undergraduate Coordinator of IGM (2013–2016)

  • Overhauled degree processes: double majors, program changes, non-major enrollment, community college articulation, handling edge cases for semester conversion, and academic action (leave of absence, suspension, probation).
  • Revamped marketing material for incoming and prospective students. Much of IGM’s web presence, publications, and external marketing still uses what I wrote.
  • Taught thousands of students how to get jobs (and continuing to do so): I created and implemented IGM’s first “co-op educational workshop” (IGME-099 in collaboration with RIT’s Career Services). I made a class that the Golisano College of Computing and Information Sciences (GCCIS) has adapted for all undergraduate majors. The material includes resumes, portfolios, interviews, career preparation, and job-hunting advice.

As Director of the Game Design Initiative at Cornell (GDIAC) (2001–2007)

  • Founded GDIAC: starting with an email to my department chair as a lecturer, I worked with staff, students, and faculty. The concept blossomed into the Ivy League’s first formal academic program in game design (gdiac.cs.cornell.edu/gdiac).
  • Developed and ran Cornell’s Minor in Game Design (www.cs.cornell.edu/undergrad/minors/game-design-minor).
  • Researched, designed, and implemented Cornell’s first game development laboratory (www.cs.cornell.edu/dis/CL3).
